Your team's task is to write a program that responds and engages in some way with Crawford and Joler's [Calculating Empires](https://calculatingempires.net/). 

The format of this creative coding project is very open-ended. It could take the form of:
- a program that takes a deep dive into some research about any 1 part of the diagram, showing original text and images like a website
- an attempt to calculate the true cost, the people and processes involved in making 1 object in the diagram from beginning to end; or comparing 2 technologies on the diagram (which is more "efficient" - and for whom?)
- a program that makes a discussion of an ethical question from the diagram interactive and visual - what does your team think of computation's role in surveillance, in generative AI, etc.?
- a speculative, "fictional" piece of software: maybe you write an interface for ordering from the Pony Express (on the diagram), a way to check on a homing pigeon (on the diagram), a tool for revealing invisible supply chains, a simulation of Schrodinger's cat or the "Golden Age of Piracy" (all from the diagram), etc. etc. - or maybe your software is from the future, what happens after the diagram?
- another original idea of your own

Goals:
- use code to express an original idea
- allow for more research on sociotechnical systems (like Crawford and Joler's diagram)
- dive into using external object-oriented programming libraries and documentation in order to make graphics, learn to build on top of existing complexity 

Requirements, your program needs to:
- use the library `raylib` in some way - https://www.raylib.com
- use both the terminal and the render window generated by `raylib`
- include at least 1 class that you write, with separate header and cpp files
- engage critically with the concepts of **abstraction**, **efficiency**, and/or **recursion** in some way
- use `CMake` as laid out in the project structure
- clearly cite *all instances* of **external sources** directly in your comments (you will probably do research for this project!)

To get started:
- first, everyone on your team should get a free github account
- 💥 when you're ready, **check out the project structure and follow the directions: https://github.com/mab253/hack2-fall26/**
- study [raylib](https://www.raylib.com/index.html), its [examples](https://www.raylib.com/examples.html) and its [documentation](https://www.raylib.com/cheatsheet/raylib_cheatsheet_v5.5.pdf). This should help you get ideas for what you can do with the library, and what functions are available to you. (NOTE: we are using raylib 5.5!)
- `raylib` is open source, so you can see [all the code](https://github.com/raysan5/raylib/blob/master/src/raylib.h) to get more details about how any of the functions work.


Suggestions:
- think about **time** and **project scope**: you only have 1 full week to decide, design, and build, including learning some new libraries. Try and decide on a theme together pretty quickly. And it is ok for the project itself to be small, but it should be thoughtful and unique.
- sketch out ideas first on paper! your topic choice and design here is an important step.
- take lessons from the first hackathon: do some work at home to be ready for the in-person time, collaborate by chatting/sharing code, and delegate and communicate

Happy coding!
